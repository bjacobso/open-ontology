01 / The workflow

A Zap is already a program.
Give it a source file.

Triggers start it. Filters decide. Actions pass data to the next system. A proposed Zapier prelude would give those familiar concepts a small, declarative vocabulary.

( ) closed-won.zap.formaProposed syntax
; Bring the connected systems into scope.
(import "@acme/sales" :as sales)
(import "@acme/billing" :as billing)
(import "@acme/workspace" :as workspace)

(zap closed-won "Welcome a new customer"
  (trigger opportunity
    (salesforce/updated sales.Opportunity))

  (filter (= opportunity.StageName "Closed Won"))

  (action customer
    (stripe/find-customer billing.account
      :salesforce-account opportunity.AccountId))

  (action notify
    (slack/send-message workspace.channels.sales
      :text (str "New customer: " customer.name))))

Import the real context

References with a home.

The sales model owns the opportunity. Billing owns the customer lookup. The workspace owns the channel. The workflow refers to those definitions.

Follow the data

Every step has a name.

opportunity feeds the filter and customer lookup. customer feeds the message. The data dependencies are visible in the source.

Keep the platform vocabulary

Still a Zap.

The proposal describes a Zapier workflow in Zapier’s terms. A future adapter would need to map these declarations to supported platform resources.

This source is a language design sketch. The imports, Zapier forms, and platform adapters shown here are proposed and cannot be executed by the current package.

02 / The connections

The lines between your apps
are part of the model.

A field mapping is a dependency. A channel is a reference. Open Ontology’s direction is to make these connections part of one checked program, across platform boundaries.
